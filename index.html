Final Mockup

The storyline and the feature set we're committing to build over the two weeks.

The Story

You're a first-year at Hogwarts, and the whole castle is counting on you. Pass Potions, survive Defence Against the Dark Arts, and read the future in Divination — except every classroom has descended into glorious chaos.

Brew, charge, cast and deliver each order before the timer runs out and the professor sweeps back in. Earn up to three stars a class, bank the points for your House, and lift the House Cup before term ends.

It plays like a wizarding kitchen under pressure: short actions, a queue of orders, and just enough chaos to keep everyone shouting across the table.

Features to implement

  • Three classrooms, each a self-contained level: Potions, Defence Against the Dark Arts, Divination.
  • Order tickets — each round hands you a queue of potion / spell / prophecy orders to clear.
  • Task pipelines: pick up → process at a station → combine → deliver, against a timer.
  • Player actions: move, run, pick up, put down, use, combine, cast.
  • Hazards per room — Cornish Pixies knock things from your hands; a Dementor chills the room and slows you.
  • Three-star rating per level, scored on speed and accuracy.
  • House points meta-progression carried across all classrooms toward the House Cup.
  • Persistent game state — points and stars survive every scene change.

How the scenes and characters connect. The player moves Main Menu → Great Hall (the hub) → into a classroom, clears ticketed orders against a timer, banks stars and points into a persistent GameState, then returns to the hub. Clear all three classes to reach the House Cup.

Scene flow with the characters that live inside each level.

Scene ↔ character relationships

  • Student (Player) — the only player-controlled character; present in every classroom and the hub.
  • Professor (NPC) — issues and receives orders inside a classroom and closes the round (e.g. Trelawney in Divination).
  • Hazards (Cornish Pixie, Dementor) — live inside a classroom level and interrupt the player; they do not cross scenes.
  • GameState — not a scene but a persistent object every scene reads from and writes to (house points + stars).
  • Great Hall (hub) — routes the player into each classroom and back, and gates the House Cup once all three are passed.

C# & Theory of CG — the automatic procedure

Procedural ticket generation. Self-implemented, no libraries. Each round's order queue is generated procedurally instead of hand-authored.

One seed value drives the whole generation, so a round is reproducible and tunable. Difficulty scales over time — more steps per order, shorter timers.

Class relationships in words

  • GameState is a persistent singleton (DontDestroyOnLoad); each LevelManager reads and writes it.
  • LevelManager owns (composition) a TaskManager and a Timer, and uses StarRating to score the round.
  • TicketGenerator feeds Tickets into TaskManager, which aggregates the active tickets.
  • PlayerController interacts with Interactable, the abstract base for Item, Station, DeliveryPoint and Hazard.
  • Station specialises into Cauldron and CrystalBallStation; Hazard into PixieAI and DementorAI.
  • AudioManager (a WWise wrapper) is used by the player, task manager and hazards to trigger spatialised SFX.
  • HUD is updated by TaskManager and Timer to show the ticket queue, star meter and clock.
